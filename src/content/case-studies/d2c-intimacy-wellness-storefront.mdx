**Note:** TEEZ is an independent personal project. It was informed by prior experience working in the intimacy and wellness e-commerce space, but the brand, storefront, and presentation shown here were rebuilt from the ground up for public portfolio use.

## Overview

TEEZ is an independent Shopify storefront concept for a direct-to-consumer intimacy and wellness brand. I built it from scratch as a public case study to explore how a premium e-commerce experience can communicate desire, trust, and product value without relying on explicit sexual language, suggestive imagery, or unsupported ingredient claims.

Intimacy and wellness brands have to communicate in a narrow space. The experience needs to feel clear, desirable, and emotionally resonant, but platforms like Google can penalize content that becomes too explicit, too erotic, visually suggestive, or too close to medical claims.

For TEEZ, I treated those limitations as a core design constraint. The storefront had to explain product value without overclaiming, create a premium brand feeling without sexualized visuals, and support conversion without using language that could put the site, blog content, product descriptions, or advertising funnels at risk.

## Project Context

This project started as a personal rebuild: a way to turn real category learnings into a fully showable storefront concept without exposing client-owned work or relying on confidential assets.

The goal was not to create a fictional landing page. It was to build the kind of storefront a real D2C intimacy and wellness brand would need to operate: homepage, product storytelling, product detail experience, reusable content sections, promotional modules, and a tone system careful enough for a sensitive advertising environment.

## Challenge

The central challenge was communication.

In this category, being too direct creates risk. During prior work in the space, we had to avoid language that spoke openly about sexuality from an erotic or pleasure-first angle, because blog content, product descriptions, and landing pages could be penalized by platforms like Google. Visual direction had similar limits: imagery could not include sexual references or suggestive framing without increasing the chance of review issues.

There was also a second layer of restriction around ingredients. Product copy had to avoid any wording that could be interpreted as a medical claim. That meant the site could not casually imply therapeutic effects, guaranteed outcomes, or health benefits that were not legally and platform-safe to claim.

So the problem was not simply “make the storefront look premium.” The real challenge was to make the product understandable, appealing, and commercially effective while staying careful, indirect, and brand-safe.

## Approach

I approached TEEZ as a storefront system built around careful clarity.

The brand needed to feel intimate without being explicit, warm without becoming vague, and confident without making risky claims. That shaped both the UX and the writing system: every section had to communicate value through tone, hierarchy, context, and product education rather than direct erotic language or aggressive benefit claims.

Instead of leaning on provocative visuals, the direction uses a more editorial and wellness-oriented language: soft pacing, controlled contrast, product-focused layouts, and copy that suggests use cases and emotional context without crossing into content that could be flagged.

The same logic informed the storefront architecture. I structured the experience around reusable sections that could support campaigns, product education, trust-building, and merchandising while keeping the wording controlled across the site.

## Solution

The storefront was designed and built as a Shopify-based e-commerce experience for a sensitive product category.

I created modular content patterns for the homepage, product storytelling, promotional sections, and product-detail experience. Each section had a specific job: introduce the brand, explain product value, reduce uncertainty, guide shoppers toward products, or support campaign messaging.

A major part of the work was the language system. Product value had to be communicated through restrained wording: clear enough for customers to understand, but careful enough to avoid erotic framing, explicit sexual references, or unsupported medical-style claims about ingredients.

That meant replacing obvious copy shortcuts with more strategic communication patterns: benefit-adjacent language, sensory but non-explicit descriptions, educational framing, softer CTAs, and product explanations grounded in experience rather than claims.

Visually, the same constraint applied. The storefront needed to feel premium and relevant to the category without relying on sexualized imagery. The design direction therefore leaned into product presentation, atmosphere, composition, and brand tone instead of explicit lifestyle cues.

## Outcome

TEEZ became a complete public case study showing how to design and build for a product category where the most obvious marketing language is often the riskiest.

The project demonstrates how I think through storefront UX, brand positioning, content systems, and platform constraints together. It is not just a visual redesign; it is a Shopify storefront concept built around the real operational problem of communicating clearly without triggering content, advertising, or compliance issues.

More importantly, it gives the portfolio a transparent way to show this type of work: an independent rebuild that captures the strategic difficulty of the category while avoiding client-owned assets, confidential details, or misleading presentation.
