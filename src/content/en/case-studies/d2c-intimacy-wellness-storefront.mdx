**Note:** TEEZ is an independent personal project. It grew out of prior experience in intimacy and wellness e-commerce, but the brand, the storefront, and everything shown here were rebuilt from scratch so the work could be shown publicly without exposing client material.

## The real problem

I designed and built the whole storefront: the UI in Figma, then the Shopify theme with its templates, its product page, and its sections. What sets it apart from any other store was not the build itself but a category constraint that ends up deciding the layout.

In intimacy and wellness the most obvious copy is the riskiest. Working in the space taught me that framing a product around pleasure, or in erotic terms, can cost Google penalties across the blog, the product pages, and the landing pages. And that any sentence reading like a medical promise about the ingredients is off limits.

That shapes the design, not only the words. Suggestive imagery falls under the same filter, so the visual direction leans on atmosphere, composition, and the product up front rather than lifestyle cues. And the templates have to hold a hierarchy that explains the product without promising anything: more room for context and education, less for the claim that would normally own the hero.

I built the sections as reusable pieces so campaigns, product education, and merchandising can change without disturbing that balance, which was the hardest part to settle.
