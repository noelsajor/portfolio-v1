**Note:** TEEZ is an independent personal project. It grew out of prior experience in intimacy and wellness e-commerce, but the brand, the storefront, and everything shown here were rebuilt from scratch so the work could be shown publicly without exposing client material.

## The real problem

In this category the most obvious copy is the riskiest. Working in the space taught me that framing a product around pleasure, or in erotic terms, can cost you Google penalties across the blog, the product pages, and the landing pages. And that any sentence reading like a medical promise about the ingredients is off limits.

So the constraint was not aesthetic, it was linguistic: selling something intimate without being able to name it directly.

The answer was to write the copy system before the storefront. Benefit-adjacent language instead of the promise, sensory descriptions that stop short of explicit, educational framing where another brand would put a claim, soft CTAs. The visual direction followed the same rule: atmosphere, composition, and the product up front, with no sexualized lifestyle cues.

The reusable Shopify sections were built on top of that, so campaigns, product education, and merchandising can all change without touching the tone, which was the hardest part to settle.
